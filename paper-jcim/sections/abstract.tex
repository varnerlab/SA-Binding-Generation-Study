% abstract.tex -- Abstract
Protein sequence generation via stochastic attention produces plausible family members from
small alignments without training, but treats all stored sequences equally and cannot direct
generation toward a designated subset of interest. We show that a single scalar parameter,
added as a bias to the sampler's attention logits, continuously shifts generation from
the full family toward a user-specified subset, with no retraining and no change to the model architecture. A practitioner supplies a small set of sequences (for example, hits
from a binding screen) and a multiplicity ratio that controls how strongly generation favors
them. The method is agnostic to what the subset represents: binding, stability, specificity,
or any other property. For unit-norm memories, the multiplicity-weighted Boltzmann target is an
exact Gaussian mixture whose component weights are the normalized multiplicities, allowing
independent equilibrium draws to benchmark the finite-step Langevin implementation. We find that
this conditioning is exact at the latent-component level, but that the decoded sequence marker can
fall short because dimensionality reduction is followed by unit normalization, finite-temperature
sampling, affine reconstruction, and argmax decoding. We term
this discrepancy the calibration gap and examine its association with a geometric measure
of how well the encoding separates the designated subset from the rest of the family.
Experiments on five Pfam families (Kunitz, SH3, WW, Homeobox, and Forkhead domains) show an
exploratory inverse association between separation and gap, one that shifts with which family
is included and that we therefore treat as a diagnostic to investigate rather than a decision
rule.
Applied to omega-conotoxin peptides associated with a calcium channel involved in pain signaling,
curated seeding from 23 designated accessions produces over a thousand candidates enriched for
the Tyr13 sequence marker and several residues reported in prior mutagenesis studies while
retaining the cysteine scaffold. These sequence and structure-model results generate hypotheses
for experimental testing; they do not establish binding. Stochastic attention can therefore
expand a user-designated sequence set into diverse candidate libraries without retraining a
generative model.
